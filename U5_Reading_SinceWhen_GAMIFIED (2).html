History of Special Effects
تاريخ المؤثرات الخاصة
🔊 Reading aloud... (0/0) 00:00
Speed: 0.9×
👆 Touch or hover any word for its Arabic translation. المسّ أي كلمة لرؤية ترجمتها

Audiences have been fascinated by moving images since December 28, 1895. The Lumière brothers showed short films in Paris. Later, Georges Méliès discovered the stop trick by accident when his camera interrupted. A bus mysteriously changed into a carriage. Filmmakers use these visual illusions to amaze viewers.

For the character King Kong (1933), filmmakers used miniatures and animation of small-scale models. George Lucas created Star Wars (1977); his crew used revolutionary innovations like a computer-controlled camera to create realistic motion and fantastic imagery.

Today, digital technology gives effects a new dimension. Films like Jurassic Park, Transformers, and Pirates of the Caribbean are references. Imaginary characters like Spiderman look realistic on television. Amazing effects will continue in the future.

Computers & Printers

The IBM 701 computer produced in 1953 was huge, slow, and took an entire room. It was successfully sold. Small PCs became widespread, and laptop computers now have many features. Mass printing started in 1440 in Germany with Gutenberg's movable type and printing press. Nowadays, high-tech printers are everywhere.

Cameras & Televisions

Digital photography started in 1975 using a cassette tape at .01 resolution. Film was introduced to the public in 1888. TV is a common household device available commercially since the 1930s.

Stoves & Cell Phones

Successful gas stove (1826) and electric stove (1891) changed kitchens. The microwave oven was invented by accident (1946). Early cell phones (1970s) were called "the brick" because they were large and heavy, but are now a necessary part of life.
